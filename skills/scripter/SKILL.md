# Scripter

One hook in, one postable script out, in the owner's voice.

## When to use
After hook-writer. The factory runs it at step 6.

## What you'll need
The winning hook, the angle block, the chosen format template, and the brief (voice, audience, offers, facts, never_say). Use the length the brief's notes or cadence set; otherwise 30 to 45 seconds.

## What makes a script work (the method)
- **Pay the hook fast.** The first value arrives by second 5. No warm-up ("so today", "hey guys", "in this video").
- **One idea.** Everything serves the payoff the hook named. Cut side points.
- **Concrete beats.** Each beat is a step, an example, a number or a picture, not a general statement.
- **Plain spoken lines.** Write how the owner talks. Short sentences, but not one-word fragments in a row. No metaphors the audience has to decode.
- **Open loop.** Keep one small question alive until the end so the viewer stays.
- **Earn the call to action.** It follows from the payoff and uses the exact live offer or keyword from the brief.
- **Say it once.** About 2.5 spoken words per second; a 40 second script is about 100 words.

## Instructions
Write the script as a table:

| Time | Spoken | On screen | Picture idea |
|---|---|---|---|

Then list: word count, estimated length, every claim with its `facts` line, and the call to action used.

Self-check before handing on: hook paid by second 5, no warm-up lines, no never_say words, voice matches the brief's examples, call to action is live.

## Rules
- Reuse the structure, never another creator's words.
- If the brief marks a claim as needing someone's confirmation, leave it out until it is in `facts`.
